02 · MVP Strategy

What to Build First

The biggest mistake builders make is building everything at once. You need to launch a version people can use — and pay for — within 8 weeks. Here's the minimum that will actually make you money.

⚠️

The MVP rule

Do NOT build the mobile app, multi-agent orchestration, Instagram integration, or analytics dashboards first. Build the thing that proves people will pay. Everything else is a feature you add after your first 10 paying customers.

✅

MVP Must-Haves

03 · Technical Stack

The Technology You Need

Every choice here is made for speed, scalability, and the Nigerian infrastructure context — including unreliable internet, WhatsApp-first usage, and cost-efficiency.

Layer Technology Why This Choice
Frontend Next.js 14 + TailwindCSS Fast, SEO-friendly landing page + React dashboard. App Router for streaming AI responses. Deploy free on Vercel.
Backend API FastAPI (Python) Fastest Python framework. Native async for WebSocket real-time chat. Easy AI library integration.
AI Engine Claude API or GPT-4o Claude is better for instructable personas and sales conversations. Use tool-calling for intent classification. Keep costs under ₦2/conversation.
WhatsApp Meta Cloud API (free) or Twilio Meta Cloud API is free at low volume. Twilio is easier to set up but costs per message. Start with Meta Cloud API.
Database PostgreSQL on Supabase Supabase gives you Postgres + auth + real-time subscriptions free tier. Reduces backend code massively.
Real-time Supabase Realtime or WebSockets Dashboard updates when new WhatsApp message arrives. Supabase makes this near-zero code.
Vector DB pgvector (inside Postgres) Store product catalogs as embeddings. Business uploads their product list → AI knows what to recommend. No separate service.
Payments Paystack Nigerian payment gateway. Naira subscriptions, card + bank transfer. Simple webhook for subscription management.
Auth Supabase Auth Email/password + Google sign-in out of the box. JWT tokens for API security. Free.
Hosting Vercel + Railway Vercel for Next.js frontend (free). Railway for FastAPI backend (cheap, auto-scaling). Both have Nigerian-proxied CDN.
File Storage Cloudinary or Supabase Storage Product images, business logos. Supabase Storage is free and already in your stack.
Email Resend Transactional emails (signup, invoice, follow-up reports). Free tier is generous.

With this stack, your total monthly infra cost for the first 100 customers is roughly $30–80/month (Railway + Supabase Pro + Cloudinary). The AI API calls are your main variable cost — budget ~$0.01–0.03 per conversation depending on message length. At 200 businesses × 100 conversations/month = $200–600/month in AI costs. You're charging ₦25k = ~$16/user/month. Your margin is very healthy.

05 · Build Phases

How to Execute in Phases

Don't try to build everything at once. This 5-phase approach gets you to your first paying customer in 6 weeks and scales from there.

1
Foundation & Infrastructure
Weeks 1–2
Set up the development environment, databases, authentication, and deploy skeletons. No features yet — just the plumbing. Getting this right means everything built on top of it is stable.
Set up Supabase project (Postgres + Auth + Storage + Realtime)
Scaffold FastAPI backend with auth middleware, CORS, and environment config
Create Next.js frontend with auth pages (login, signup, onboarding)
Register Meta WhatsApp Business API app and get test credentials
Set up Paystack account and test subscription webhook
Deploy backend to Railway, frontend to Vercel with CI/CD
2
Core AI & WhatsApp Integration
Weeks 3–4
This is the heart of your product. A message comes in on WhatsApp, the AI reads it, understands what the customer wants, and replies intelligently using the business's product information.
Build WhatsApp webhook receiver (Meta Cloud API verification + message handling)
Build the AI Sales Agent prompt — this is where most of your time should go. Your AI needs a strong system prompt that makes it act like a trained salesperson for any business
Build product catalog ingestion — business uploads a CSV or types products, embeddings stored in pgvector
Implement intent classification (buying intent, support, pricing, complaint) to route correctly
Build auto follow-up scheduler: if a lead goes cold for 24 hours, AI sends a warm follow-up
Test end-to-end: WhatsApp message → AI reply → lead saved in database
3
Dashboard & Business Features
Weeks 5–6

Human takeover: business owner can jump into a conversation and AI steps back
Onboarding flow: business connects WhatsApp, uploads products, configures AI personality
Web chat widget (embeddable script) for business websites
Basic analytics: daily chats, new leads, conversion rate
4

Integrate Paystack subscription billing — Starter (free), Growth (₦25k/mo), Business (custom)
Build plan limits enforcement (message limits on free tier, feature gating)
Launch landing page with demo video, waitlist, and pricing
Onboard 10 beta businesses from your network — WhatsApp groups, Twitter/X, markets
Set up customer support channel (a WhatsApp group will do at this stage)
Create a feedback loop — weekly check-in call or voice note with beta users

08 · Go-to-Market

How to Get Your First 100 Customers

Building the product is only half the job. Distribution is the other half — and most builders neglect it. Here's a realistic plan to get paying customers without a big marketing budget.

Start in WhatsApp business groups
There are thousands of active trade groups — fashion sellers, food vendors, electronics dealers. Join them, provide value, then share what you're building. Don't spam. Be helpful first.
Record a 90-second demo video
Show a real business getting a WhatsApp message and the AI handling it perfectly. Post it on Twitter/X, LinkedIn, TikTok with the caption: "This is why you're losing sales at midnight." Let the video do the selling.
Partner with business consultants & brand managers
Find people who already consult for SMBs and offer them 20–30% commission for every business they bring on. They have the trust and relationships. You have the product.

Techpoint.Africa, Disrupt Africa, Nairaland's tech section, StartupSA groups. A good Product Hunt launch can get you 500+ signups in a day. Prepare a great demo.
Offer free setup to 10 Instagram sellers
Find 10 businesses with 10k+ Instagram followers who are clearly active in DMs. Offer to set them up free for 30 days. If it converts 3 more sales for them, they'll pay and tell others.
Write SEO content for Google searches
"How to automate WhatsApp replies for your business in Nigeria", "Best WhatsApp CRM for small businesses", "AI chatbot for Nigerian businesses". These searches happen every day. Own them.
Build in public on Twitter/X
Share your journey — weekly updates, subscriber milestones, product wins and failures. Nigerian tech Twitter is very active. A small, engaged following of founders and business owners is better than 100k random followers.
Attend SMB-focused events & trade fairs
Lagos Fashion Week vendors, Alaba electronics market, Balogun textile market, MTC events. These are your customers in the flesh. Bring a phone with a live demo. ₦25k/month will sound like nothing after they see a demo.

10 · Pitfalls to Avoid

What Will Kill This Project

Most startups don't fail because the product is bad. They fail for predictable, avoidable reasons. Here's what will sink SmartSales AI if you're not careful.

Building without talking to customers
Before you write a single line of code, have 20 conversations with WhatsApp business owners. Ask: "What's your biggest frustration with how you handle customer messages?" If their answers don't match your product idea, pivot before it's too late.
Waiting until it's "perfect" to launch
A messy, broken product in front of 10 real customers is worth more than a perfect product nobody has seen. Launch at 60% ready. Fix it based on what real users tell you.
WhatsApp API policy violations
Meta has strict rules — no spam, no unsolicited messages, no bulk sending to cold contacts. If your customers use SmartSales to spam, Meta will ban their WhatsApp Business account AND your API access. Build compliance into your product from day one. Add message guidelines to onboarding.
Ignoring AI quality
If the AI gives wrong product prices or rude replies, the business owner will lose a customer and blame you. Person 3's job is not just "connect the API" — it's crafting a sales AI that behaves like a trained, professional salesperson. Test it with 100+ real conversation scenarios before any customer uses it.
Competing on price instead of value
Don't race to be the cheapest. Compete on results. If a business makes ₦200,000 more per month because SmartSales is closing leads they were losing, ₦25,000/month is trivial. Sell the outcome, not the feature list.
Not retaining customers
SaaS lives and dies on churn. If you're signing up 20 customers/month but losing 18, you'll never grow. Set up check-in calls at day 7, day 30, and day 60. If someone stops logging in, reach out that same day. Retention is more important than acquisition.
Building too many features too early
Every feature you add before you have 50 customers is a gamble. Build only what existing customers are actively asking for. Everything else is a distraction from finding product-market fit.
🔑

The single most important thing

The quality of your AI sales agent's system prompt is 80% of your product's value. A generic chatbot that says "Thanks for your message! Here are our products:" will not retain customers. An AI that greets customers by name, references what they last asked about, creates urgency, handles objections, and books follow-ups — that's what businesses will pay for and tell their friends about. Invest deeply in this.
